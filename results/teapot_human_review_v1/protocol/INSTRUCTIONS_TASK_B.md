# Task B — reviewer instructions

Thank you for coming back for the second stage. This should take about 15–25
minutes, and you can stop and resume whenever you like.

You will see **20 pairs** of images and answer **one question** about each.
There are no right or wrong answers, nothing is timed, and there is no expected
or preferred outcome.

## Two things to know before you start

**1. This stage tells you which image is which.** In every pair, one image is
labelled **REFERENCE** (the original model's output) and the other
**CANDIDATE** (the modified model's output). That is deliberate — the
comparison needs it — but it means this stage is only **partially masked**, and
you will know the reference's identity while judging.

**2. Please leave your Task A answers exactly as you submitted them.** If
anything here makes you want to revise a Task A answer, please don't change it —
just mention it in the optional explanation box. This viewer cannot read or
alter your Task A answers, and nothing you do here affects them.

## How to open it

1. Download the ZIP and **extract it fully** (don't work inside the zip
   preview).
2. Open `index.html` from the extracted folder in your browser.

It runs **entirely offline**: no account, no login, no upload, no tracking, no
network of any kind. Your answers stay in your own browser until you choose to
export a file.

## What you are judging

You are comparing **structural deformation only** — how well each image holds
together as a rendered scene. Judge only what you can see, and please don't try
to guess the text prompt.

Please keep these in mind:

* **The reference may already contain defects of its own.** It is not a
  guaranteed-clean image and not a standard of perfection.
* **A changed layout or composition is not automatically deterioration.** The
  two images may simply show different scenes.
* **A coherent substitution is not automatically deterioration.** If the
  candidate shows some other object rendered cleanly, that is not by itself
  structural damage.
* **A missing teapot is not automatically deterioration.** Absence of the object
  is not the same as the image falling apart.
* The two images in a pair started from **matched initial noise**, but matched
  noise **does not guarantee identical composition**. Expect genuinely different
  pictures.

## The question

For each pair:

> **Does the candidate show less, about the same, or more structural deformation
> than the reference — or can you not fairly compare them?**

* **Less** deformation than the reference
* **About the same**
* **More** deformation than the reference
* **I cannot fairly compare these two**

**"I cannot fairly compare" is a real answer**, not a cop-out. Please use it
whenever the two images are too different to be judged against each other. It
counts as a completed answer.

There is also a short optional explanation box. Use it if something is worth
noting; leave it blank otherwise.

Each image is shown at native 512×512 and **zooms independently of the other**,
so you can inspect either side closely.

## Before you start

You'll be asked for:

* **Your reviewer alias** — please use the **same alias you used for Task A**.
* **A presentation order** — the default derives an order from your alias.
  Either option is fine.
* **Three required declarations.** The first asks whether you completed and
  exported your final Task A file before starting Task B. The other two ask
  about the period **before you started Task A, including earlier that same
  day** — whether you had seen condition labels for these images, or anyone gave
  you image-specific guidance.

Learning which image is the reference **in this Task B stage** is expected and
is not a problem — that is how this stage works, and it is recorded
automatically. The declarations ask only about what you knew **before Task A**.

## Saving, resuming and sending your answers

* Progress saves in your browser automatically, and there's a **Save** button.
* To stop and come back: reopen `index.html` and enter the **same alias** —
  you'll be offered **Resume**.
* **Moving to another computer?** Export a draft first, then on the new machine
  use **"Continue from an exported Task B file"** on the first screen. It
  restores that file's own alias, declarations, order and answers.
* Under **Finish & export**:
  * **Export draft** at any time to back up partial work.
  * **Export final** once all 20 pairs are answered.
* Send the exported JSON file back however you were asked to.

Please complete Task B **independently** — without discussing specific images
with anyone else who is reviewing them.

## A note on what this is

These are AI-generated images being assessed descriptively. Your answers will be
reported as what reviewers said they saw, alongside the other reviewer's raw
answers. There is no expected result and no answer pattern is being looked for.
