# T2I concept unlearning — findings of 2026-10-27

Companion to the PI deck already in `deck/`. Everything here is **development data**; no
confirmatory protocol was frozen, so no confirmatory claim is available regardless of outcome.
Base model SD-1.4 (`CompVis/stable-diffusion-v1-4`, rev `133a221b…`) throughout.

Read `docs/PI_DISCUSSION_2026-10-27.md` first — it is the decision memo. `docs/MANDATE_v2_2026-10-27.md`
is the governing protocol.

## 1. The central result

Across **86 concept-matched arms** — the attacked concept is the one the arm erased, taken from each
arm's own evaluation record where it has one (55) and otherwise from its build record (31) — on 5
concepts (church 70, parachute 7, Van Gogh 4, garbage truck 3, tench 2):

> **No arm has been *shown* to both suppress its target and resist relearning.** 56 are driven to
> closed-set target accuracy H = 1.000 by an attack of at most 25 gradient steps. 21 cannot be read
> at all (their defence lives in the text encoder and our post-attack evaluation loaded the base
> one — a defect we documented). 6 were destroyed or heavily degraded (un-attacked retain8 0.0000 to
> 0.3417 against the teacher's 0.9417). 3 never erased the target at all (un-attacked target
> accuracy 1.0000). **Every arm is accounted for; no category is left open.**

### 1.1 The published baselines, at matched attacker effort

Closed on 2026-10-27 (study U21). Previously ESD and adapted-IMMA had a single legacy learning rate
and UCE and GIFT had **no** 25-step cells, so no baseline comparison met MANDATE §5's
matched-effort requirement. Now 3 rates x 3 seeds x 25 steps each, evidence schema, 35/36 cells
gate-valid:

| arm | H before attack | **max-over-valid H after** | retained capability after |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `esd_church` | 0.47 - 0.73 | **1.0000** | 0.88 - 1.00 |
| `gift_church_b1.0` | 0.40 - 0.47 | **1.0000** (all three rates) | 0.97 - 1.00 |
| `uce_church` | 0.00 - 0.07 | **1.0000** | 0.96 - 1.00 |
| `imma_church_frp_a1e-7_s1500` | 0.07 - 0.13 | **1.0000** | 0.94 - 0.97 |

UCE and IMMA begin **well** erased (H 0.00-0.13), are driven to 1.000, and **keep their utility**
afterwards. The reversal is not bought by wrecking the model: the attacker gains the concept and the
model remains usable.

### 1.2 Second concept, and the K>0 arms

- **U19** (`docs/STUDY_U19_second_concept.md`): both parachute arms reach max-over-valid H = 1.000.
  The *result* transfers to a second concept; the attacker *calibration* does not.
- **U20** (`docs/STUDY_U20_K8_sweep.md`): the three K=8 builds read 0.9333 / 0.9333 / 0.8000 when
  measured at one learning rate and **1.000** once two more were added. One went from H_before
  0.0000 to H_after 1.0000 in 25 steps. They were under-measured, not robust.

## 2. Measurement-validity findings

Full detail and supporting cells: `docs/FINDINGS_MEASUREMENT_2026-10-27.md`. All derived from
records that already existed, at **zero marginal GPU cost**.

1. **A negative "relearning rise" has two distinct causes that must not be merged** — saturation on
   an arm that never erased, versus a model the attacker *destroyed* (target panel and retained
   capability both driven to ~0). Reported naively, a destroyed cell reads as near-perfect erasure
   that no attack could undo.
2. **The attacker's useful learning-rate window is arm-dependent**, and its apparent breakdown point
   is partly an arithmetic artifact of the evaluation seed, since `T_rise = T_after - T_before`.
   There is no cheap predictor: the hypothesis that it tracks pre-attack fragility was tested and
   **refuted**.
3. **Pre/post difference metrics are noise-dominated.** On a byte-identical checkpoint the
   pre-attack score is 0.2000 on one evaluation seed and 0.8000 on another, so the delta swings 5.5x
   while the post-attack score is identical. Report the post-attack value; it reproduces to within
   one quantum (1/15) across independent studies.
4. **An absolute post-attack utility drop cannot evidence a booby-trap.** Over 95 gate-valid cells
   the mean change is -0.0268, but 30.5% lose more than 0.05 and the worst loses 0.7708 — on arms
   with no trap mechanism, including the plain teacher. Such a claim needs a *paired* comparison at
   matched attacker settings.
5. **Closed-set CLIP accuracy is not image quality** — three methods visibly destroy an unrelated
   concept while scoring 0.87-0.97 on it.
6. **A reproducibility limit in an upstream artifact**: the pinned Meta-Unlearning repository, whose
   paper is titled *Preventing Relearning Unlearned Concepts*, ships no relearning-attack script and
   none of the three datasets its code loads, so the attacker configuration behind its headline
   claim is not recoverable from the artifact. This is an observation about the released code, **not**
   a claim that the paper is wrong.

**What these findings are not:** they are demonstrated on our pipeline and our arms. They show that
a robustness claim measured without these guardrails is *unsupported* — not that any specific
published claim is false.

## 3. The trap direction, and why it closed

The one remaining mechanism class with a published instance is a **trap**: make fine-tuning on the
target destroy retained capability. Three successive attempts, each correcting a divergence from the
authors' released code that we found by measurement:

| stage | instrument | mean parameter drift | reading |
|---|---|---:|---|
| reference: coherent motion | — | 1.5e-3 (1500 steps x lr) | — |
| reference: pure random walk | — | 3.87e-5 | — |
| **S1** (`meta_v2`) | difference estimated on **independent random draws** | 2.27e-5 | gradient was pure draw noise. The no-op arm's logged gap matched the active arm's to four significant figures |
| **S1b** (`meta_v3`) | + common random numbers, upstream's fixed meta timesteps, Adam eps 1e-12 | 2.05e-5 | estimator verified correct (the measured gap fell 0.190 -> 3e-6, below the loss's own fp32 resolution) but the gradient direction was resampled each step |
| **S1c** (`meta_v4`) | + 8-sample micro-batched meta gradient (upstream ships batch 10; we had 1) | **6.10e-6** | averaging reduced the norm by sqrt(8), so the per-sample gradients are near-uncorrelated. Drift fell **below** the random walk |

A stopping rule was declared before S1c ran and **enforced in the runner**, which measured its own
drift and refused to produce a panel or any attack cell in the stop band. S1c landed there, so the
direction is **closed as untestable within our resources**. No fourth instrumentation fix.

### 3.1 What separates the two halves of the objective

S1b's `gamma_f` arm is the one build in the stage whose drift exceeded a random walk (7.58e-5, ~2x),
so its verdict is readable rather than inconclusive:

- the **`gamma_f` level term** ("keep the target erased after the attack") has real purchase, costs
  utility (retain8 0.9417 -> 0.8583, all-nontarget 0.9212 -> 0.8061), and produces **no trap
  effect** — a genuine negative;
- the **zeta difference term**, which *is* the trap, never moved a model above random walk under any
  of the three instruments and remains **untested**.

### 3.2 What this licenses saying, and what it does not

**Licensed:** we could not make this trap mechanism displace an 859,520,964-parameter UNet at a
~1500-sample budget with a first-order estimator, having corrected every configuration difference we
found against the released code. That is a statement about practicality at our scale.

**Not licensed:** any claim that the published method fails, or that traps cannot work. Our port has
no `hrm` pool, carries two weights where upstream has five, and simulates a different attacker. The
authors train at batch 10 for 100 steps on three curated pools with five tuned weights. A null from
our instrument is not evidence about theirs.

## 4. Protocol corrections that changed a verdict

Documented in `docs/PREREG_S1_AMENDMENT_1_2026-10-27.md`:

- The success criterion was originally an **absolute** post-attack utility drop >= 0.25. That is
  routinely produced by an ordinary attack at a high learning rate against an arm with no trap —
  including the plain teacher at -0.3958 on a gate-valid, non-destroyed cell. Replaced before the
  attack phase ran with a **paired difference against a matched control at the same learning rate
  and seed, required at two or more rates**.
- That change **blocked a concrete false positive.** S1's `gamma_f` arm reaches a paired trap effect
  of **+0.2708 at one learning rate**, above the 0.25 bar — carried by a single seed (+0.4792 against
  +0.0625), inside the control's own 0.375 seed spread at high attacker rates, on a model that had
  drifted only 3.68e-5, and with `H_after` = 1.0000 so the attacker took the concept anyway. The
  two-rate requirement rejected it. Under the original criterion S1 would have reported a trap.
- The paired metric's noise floor was then **measured** rather than assumed: the byte-identical
  no-op arm reads within +/-0.02 typical and +/-0.042 worst, so the 0.25 threshold sits ~12x above
  it.
