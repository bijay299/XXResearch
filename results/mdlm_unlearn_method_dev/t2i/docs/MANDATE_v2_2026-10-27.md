# MANDATE v2 — amended 2026-10-27 after PI feedback

**This is the authoritative mandate. It supersedes the prior objective wherever they conflict.**
Prior mandate (v1): *"develop, implement and rigorously evaluate an unlearning algorithm that
outperforms the strongest relevant existing methods on a clearly defined forgetting–utility
trade-off."*

## 1. The amended objective

Selective concept unlearning in text-to-image diffusion models: **suppress the target concept,
preserve useful generation, and make the suppression resistant to subsequent fine-tuning.**

The bar is a **clear, defensible advantage over existing methods**, satisfied by any of:

| route | what it requires |
|---|---|
| **stronger resistance** | meaningfully better robustness at comparable cost and utility |
| **lower cost** | comparable useful performance at substantially less computation or complexity |
| **better trade-off** | an explicitly measured, justified improvement among forgetting, robustness, quality and cost |

**Explicitly not sufficient:** similar performance at similar or greater cost. A cheaper method that
fails just as completely is **not** a contribution. "Comparable performance" requires positive
evidence and a stated tolerance; failure to detect a difference is not evidence of equivalence.

## 2. What this supersedes

| superseded | by |
|---|---|
| v1's fixed bar `retain8 >= 0.85 AND max-over-valid H <= 0.40` as *the* success definition | per-experiment success criteria declared before the decisive run (§4), against a named comparator |
| "preserve/improve UTRAP" as an implicit goal | **UTRAP has no protected status.** Its name, mechanism and machinery may be retired (§3) |
| treating a single attacker learning rate as a robustness measurement | robustness is the **maximum over a calibrated attacker sweep** (§5) |
| the retain-at-attacked-endpoint direction as "the one open direction" | **withdrawn as unmotivated** (§3.3) and replaced by its inverse (§5.2) |

Preserved unchanged: GPU 0–1 only via the reservation mechanism, at most two simultaneous GPU jobs,
storage within the 500 GiB authorization on a shared volume, no paid external services, MDLM
untouched, and all completed evidence and reproducible artifacts retained.

### 3.2 The decision

**Retire the K>0 attack-anticipation machinery.** It is more expensive, delivers no measurable
benefit, and is subsumed by published work. The K=0 configuration is retained only as a *baseline*
under its accurate description — ascent-plus-retain-anchor — not as a method claim.

Artifacts are preserved: all builds, all 1,660 result records, the evidence snapshot, the figures
and the PI deck remain in place and reproducible. Retiring the direction does not delete its
evidence.

### 3.3 The direction also withdrawn, and why

"Preserve retain performance at the attacked endpoint" was identified as the one gap unoccupied by
the four surveyed upstreams. **It is unmotivated in this threat model:** across 45 evidence-complete
cells, post-attack retained capability *rises* (mean +0.0231, median 0.0000); only 3 of 45 cells lost
more than 0.05. The attacker fine-tunes on target images with an ordinary diffusion loss, which
restores general capability as a side effect. There is nothing to preserve.

## 4. What the evidence implies about where an advantage could come from

Finding 3 and 4 together say the failure is **structural, not a matter of degree**. Every method
tested only *moves theta*; the attacker fine-tunes the full UNet on target images and walks it back,
and doing more damage to the starting point does not help. **A defence that only displaces theta
cannot win in this threat model.**

That leaves mechanisms that change **what fine-tuning can do**, not where the model sits:

| class | mechanism | status here |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Trap / self-destruct** | fine-tuning on the concept triggers collapse of retained capability, so the attacker recovers the concept but loses the model | published precedent (Meta-Unlearning ICCV 2025; CTRAP in LLMs). **Our port omitted two of the paper's three objective terms, so the published mechanism has never been fairly tested here.** |
| **B. Capacity removal** | delete the representational capacity so relearning must rebuild it | partially tested and **failing**: FRAG/FRP prunes 4.4M weights to zero and is fully reversed; the network re-learns through remaining capacity |
| **C. Data-side poisoning** | make the attacker's own fine-tuning data drive the model away from the concept | speculative, no precedent located, deprioritised |

**Class A is the only class with a concrete mechanism that targets the fine-tuning process itself,
and the only one with an untested published instance in our possession.**

### 4.1 The success criterion this implies, declared before the decisive run

A trap does not prevent recovery; it makes recovery **cost** the attacker. Our evaluation already
measures the relevant quantity, and the baseline is close to zero cost.

> **Claim to be tested:** at comparable pre-attack erasure and utility, a trap mechanism makes a
> successful relearning attack destroy the model's retained capability, where existing methods cost
> the attacker nothing.

| **primary metric** | change in retained capability from before to after a *successful* (gate-valid) attack |

Post-attack retain is on average unchanged but **high-variance, driven by attacker lr** |
| **minimum worthwhile improvement** | ~~post-attack retained capability drops by **>= 0.25** on gate-valid attacks~~ **SUPERSEDED 2026-10-27:** an absolute >= 0.25 drop is routinely produced by an ordinary attack at lr 1e-4 on arms with **no trap at all**, including the FRAG/FRP teacher (-0.3958), so the absolute threshold yields false positives. **Equivalence tolerance,** where "comparable" is claimed: pre-attack target suppression and retain8
within **0.067** (one unit of the metric's 1/15 quantum) of the comparator, with the comparison made
over >=3 builds, not inferred from a single pair.

## 5. Protocol changes, binding from now

1. **Robustness is the maximum over a calibrated attacker sweep.** A single learning rate is a lower
   bound on recovery and may not be reported as a robustness estimate. Attacker effectiveness is
   non-monotone in learning rate, so a larger rate is not a stronger attack.

   **The measured curve (church, 25 steps, U9 + U18, 3 seeds per rung):**

   | attacker lr | FRAG/FRP teacher, mean H | UTRAP K=0 b0, mean H |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | 1e-5 | 0.867 | 0.267 |
   | 2e-5 | 0.933 | 0.467 |
   | 3e-5 | 0.978 | 0.778 |
   | **5e-5** | **1.000** | **0.933** |
   | 7e-5 | 0.800 | 0.667 |
   | 1e-4 | 0.689 | 0.600 |
   | 2e-4 | 0/3 valid | 0/3 valid |

   Both arms peak at **5e-5**, so the optimum is a property of the attack configuration rather than
   of the defence, and a sweep is transferable across arms. The curve's steepness tracks erasure
   depth: the barely-erased teacher is nearly flat at the low end while the heavily-erased arm
   climbs steeply, which is why a fixed low learning rate understates recovery most on exactly the
   arms that look best.

   **Bounded range: 1e-5 to 1e-4 at this budget.** 2e-4 is outside it for every arm tested.

1b. **A negative `T_rise` is the signature of an attacker outside its useful range**, and is a
   sharper diagnostic than the gate threshold alone. At lr 2e-4 the FRAG/FRP teacher gives
   `T_rise` of **-0.2083, -0.1667 and -0.1250** on three seeds: the attack *degrades* the model's
   ability to generate the target on the attacker's own training prompts. Reported in isolation such
   a cell reads as "the attack made the defence stronger" (H fell from 0.1333 to 0.0667 on seed 0).
   It is nothing of the kind.

   **Refinement (same date, from a re-analysis of all 123 evidence-complete 25-step cells):
   `T_rise < 0` alone is not sufficient to label a cell out-of-range.** Eleven cells have
   `T_rise < 0`, and they fall into two categories that must not be merged:

   | mode | `T_before` | signature | n | reading |
   |---|---|---|---:|---|
   | **saturation** | >= 0.90 | small negative rise, `T_after` still high | 1 | the target was never suppressed on this arm, so there is no headroom for a rise; the negative value is noise at ceiling and says **nothing about the attacker** |
   | **destroyed** | < 0.90 | `T_after` -> ~0 **and** post-attack retained capability -> ~0 | 10 | attacker outside its useful range |

   The one saturation cell is `u4_k0_s400_b1` attacked on **tench** (a trainability control, never
   erased): `T_before` 1.0000, `T_rise` -0.0417. Under the unrefined rule it would have been
   reported as an out-of-range attacker, which is wrong in the opposite direction.
   **Binding rule: a cell is out-of-range only if `T_rise < 0` with `T_before < 0.90`, confirmed by
   collapsed post-attack retained capability.** Such cells are excluded from robustness estimates.

1c. **The attacker's useful window is arm-dependent, so a learning rate calibrated on one arm is
   not transferable to another — including other arms of the same concept.** Measured on parachute
   `p_r3` (25 steps, 3 seeds, `outputs/atk_U19EV_p_r3_*`):

   | attacker lr | valid cells | max-over-valid H | destroyed cells |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | **1e-5** | 3/3 | **1.000** | 0 |
   | 5e-5 | 2/3 | 1.000 | 0 |
   | 1e-4 | 1/3 | 1.000 | 2 |
   | 2e-4 | 0/3 | — | 3 |

   Two things vary by arm. **Where the attacker saturates:** the church teacher needs 5e-5 to reach
   H = 1.000 and is at 0.867 at 1e-5, whereas `p_r3` is already at 1.000 at 1e-5. **Where it breaks
   down:** first destroyed cell at 1e-4 for both church `frp_r3` and parachute `p_r3`, but at
   **5e-5** for parachute `p_r5`. So the sweep must extend **below** a previously-found optimum as
   well as above it. A fixed 1e-4 chosen from church would report `H = 0.3333` and `0.0000` on
   parachute from models it had destroyed, as though they were defence.

   **Qualification added after U19 completed (24/24), weakening the across-arm part of this
   clause.** The breakdown onset is **partly an arithmetic artifact of the evaluation seed**, not
   purely a property of the arm. `T_rise = T_after - T_before`, so when a high-lr attack drives
   `T_after` toward 0, a *larger* `T_before` crosses `T_rise < 0` at a *lower* learning rate. The
   prediction is deductive and the data match it in all six U19 (arm, seed) pairs without exception:

   | arm | seed | `T_before` | first lr reading DESTROYED |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | `p_r3` | 6 | 0.5417 | 2e-4 |
   | `p_r3` | 1 | 0.6250 | 1e-4 |
   | `p_r3` | 0 | 0.6250 | 1e-4 |
   | `p_r5` | 6 | 0.5000 | 2e-4 |
   | `p_r5` | 0 | 0.5417 | 1e-4 |
   | `p_r5` | 1 | **0.7083** | **5e-5** |

   Within each arm, onset is monotone in `T_before`. `p_r5`'s apparently earlier breakdown is driven
   by its seed-1 cell, whose `T_before` of 0.7083 is the highest in the study — and clause 7
   establishes that `T_before`-type pre-attack quantities move by up to 0.6000 on a byte-identical
   checkpoint purely from the evaluation seed.

   At **matched** `T_before` only one comparison is available: at 0.5417, `p_r3` seed 6 first breaks
   at 2e-4 while `p_r5` seed 0 breaks at 1e-4, which is consistent with `p_r5` being genuinely more
   fragile (its pre-attack retained capability is 0.75-0.85 against `p_r3`'s 0.92-0.98). **That is
   one matched pair, so the across-arm difference is *suggested*, not established**, and §2.1's
   refutation of fragility as a general predictor still stands on the church arms.

   **Unifying consequence.** `T_rise` and `delta_H` are both *differences* against a pre-attack
   quantity, and both inherit that quantity's evaluation-seed noise. Clause 7 already replaces
   `delta_H` with `H_after`. The same logic applies here: **classify cells by the absolute
   post-attack quantities — `T_after` together with post-attack retained capability — and treat
   `T_rise` as serving only its narrow gate function** (did the attacker learn what it was given),
   read with headroom in mind. The gate must stay a rise, since an absolute `T_after` threshold
   cannot distinguish "the attacker relearned the concept" from "the model always generated it".

   **Two claims made earlier on this axis are withdrawn.** (a) "The failure mode is worse on the
   second concept" — withdrawn. A negative rise is bounded in magnitude by `T_before`, so raw
   magnitudes are not comparable across arms. Normalised, the two concepts are indistinguishable:
   mean `T_rise / T_before` is **-0.667** on church (n=4) and **-0.651** on parachute (n=6), with
   mean `T_after` 0.0729 and 0.2153 and mean post-attack retained capability 0.1406 and 0.0729.
   Both are driven to the same place; church merely had less room to fall (`T_before` 0.21 vs 0.63).
   (b) "The useful range is *concept*-dependent" — withdrawn in favour of *arm*-dependent, since the
   only arm that shifts breakdown onset (`p_r5`) differs from `p_r3` within one concept.

   **A tempting shortcut was tested and refuted.** Hypothesis: the usable learning-rate ceiling
   tracks the defended model's pre-attack retained capability (its fragility). Refuted by the
   existing cells — `u16_k0_lam0.6_b0` at retained capability **0.5000**, the most fragile arm
   measured, has no destroyed cell up to 1e-4, while arms at 0.92-0.96 do. There is no cheap
   predictor; the window has to be measured per arm.

7. **Report `H_after`, not `delta_H`.** The pre-attack H panel is regenerated per attack seed, so
   `H_before` is not a property of the checkpoint. The mechanism is in
   `scripts/attack_relearn_v2.py`: `scored_panel` seeds each image's generator as
   `attack_seed*1000 + 101*concept_index + prompt_index`, so changing the attack seed redraws the
   whole evaluation panel. Within a cell the before and after panels are *paired* on identical
   generator seeds, so the instability is not unpaired-noise sloppiness — it is that **`H_after`
   saturates near ceiling on every arm tested, leaving `delta_H` to inherit all of `H_before`'s
   variance.** Verified not to be checkpoint mutation: every U19 cell records
   `provenance.input_unet_sha256`, and across all cells each defence checkpoint resolves to exactly
   **one** distinct hash (`frp_parachute_r5_l05` → `4edd2be48057…`, `frp_parachute_r3_l05` →
   `5ae7fdb3f98b…`). The checkpoints are bit-identical across seeds. Measured on that single
   unchanged checkpoint
   (`adapters/frp_parachute_r5_l05/unet.pt`), `H_before` is **0.2000** on seed 0 and **0.8000** on
   seed 1 — range 0.6000, i.e. 9 of 15 images. `H_after` on those same two cells is **0.9333 and
   0.9333**, identical. So `delta_H` swings 0.7333 vs 0.1333 — a factor of 5.5 — from the
   evaluation seed alone, with the checkpoint, attacker learning rate, step count and budget all
   held fixed. On `p_r3` the same instability is present and smaller (12 cells, `H_before` range
   0.2000).

   Consequences, binding: (a) **`delta_H` at n=1 is noise and may not be reported as an effect**;
   (b) the primary robustness quantity stays **max-over-valid `H_after`**, which is what
   FINDING35 §4's ratchet already uses and which is seed-stable here to 0.0000; (c) a checkpoint's
   erasure depth may not be characterised from one cell — `p_r5` was described in this project's
   own notes as "genuinely erased at 0.2000" on the strength of a single seed, and that
   characterisation is **withdrawn**; (d) this is a *different and larger* quantity than the
   previously documented ~2/15 H noise, which was measured on `H_after` with the attack trajectory
   held identical, not on `H_before` across evaluation seeds. Both stand.
2. **Gate-invalid cells are inconclusive, never defence.** Report counts and both conditional and
   unconditional summaries.
3. **Cost is measured, not inferred.** Wall-clock, peak memory and samples processed. Equal outer
   updates are *not* equal computation.
4. **Separate independent defence builds from attack seeds from generated images** in every count.
5. **Quality alongside recognition.** Report prompt adherence and saved images; closed-set CLIP
   accuracy is not an image-quality measure and has been shown here to call a visibly destroyed
   image "preserved".
6. **Account for differing starting points.** Methods do not begin the attack from the same erasure
   level; comparisons must match on it or state the mismatch.

### 5.2 Replacing the withdrawn direction

The withdrawn direction aimed to *preserve* retain at the attacked endpoint. The evidence says
attacks already restore utility, so the research question inverts: **can a defence make successful
relearning destroy retained capability?** That is class A, and §4.1 is its success criterion.

## 6. Stage plan, with stopping rules

| stage | work | continue if | stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0** implementation check (CPU) | reconcile our Meta-Unlearning port against the paper's three-term objective; identify exactly what is missing | the missing terms are implementable within our harness | the published objective needs data or scale we cannot supply |
| **S1** decisive pilot, n=1 | full three-term objective, one build, church. Measure: does utility survive *normal* use, and does a gate-valid attack collapse it | utility survives un-attacked **and** post-attack retain drops >= 0.25 | the trap fires on normal use, or post-attack retain drop < 0.10 |
| **S2** replication, n=3 builds | three independent builds, full attacker sweep, cost measured | the S1 effect holds at n=3 with the sweep | the effect is inside build spread |
| **S3** generalisation | second concept, prompts not used for selection, baselines at matched attacker effort | effect holds | it does not transfer |
| **S4** independent review | mathematical rationale, novelty mapping against Meta-Unlearning/CTRAP/MLAC, decisive-evidence audit | — | — |

**Budget:** S0 free, S1 <= 3 GPU-hours, S2 <= 8 GPU-hours, S3 <= 10 GPU-hours. If S1 and S2 both
fail, class A is retired and the honest report is that no tested approach meets the mandate.

## 7. Honest statement of risk

Three method families and six method instances have now been fully reversed under calibrated attack,
including at utility levels no deployment would accept. It is entirely possible that **no approach
within our resources meets this mandate.** If so, the result will be reported as such. The
calibrated-attacker protocol and the negative result are real findings regardless, but they are not
what the PI asked for and will not be presented as if they were.
