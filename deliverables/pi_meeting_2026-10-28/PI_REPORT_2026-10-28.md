# Recommendation

**Retire the method direction and write the structural result.**

Across **164 erased models** and **1,215 attack measurements**, no re-implementation of any concept-erasure method survives relearning — including the four built specifically to resist it and both of our own. Three of four published methods are undone by a **single gradient step**. The concept returns under **benign fine-tuning with no adversary at all**, verified in the images.

**One exception, found this week.** ResAlign (NeurIPS 2025) — the authors' own released checkpoint — holds far longer than anything else. We reproduced their un-attacked figure exactly, verified it against a working control, and attacked it: it resists at 25 and 100 gradient steps where every other method falls at one to four, and breaks only by 300. It does not make erasure permanent; it makes the attack roughly **100× more expensive**.

That exception makes the paper stronger, not weaker. **Four families of method fail for one structural reason; a fifth escapes it, and we can say precisely what it does differently.**

**What we need from you:** a decision on this framing, and on how to spend the next ten days.

## 1. It comes back with no adversary

Fine-tuning on **golf balls, french horns, cassette players and gas pumps** — no church prompt, no church image, no attacker. 200 steps, n=3, all cells valid.

| method | suppression before | after benign fine-tuning | survives the control? | verified in images? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|

# The problem, and the bar

We set out to make a text-to-image diffusion model forget a concept in a way that survives someone fine-tuning it afterwards. Three requirements, in order:

1. the concept does not appear;
2. everything else still works;
3. both hold after an adversary retrains the weights.

The bar you set was explicit: *a clear, defensible advantage over existing methods. More computation is acceptable if the results are meaningfully better; comparable results are acceptable if substantially cheaper or simpler. An explicitly measured trade-off counts. Similar performance at similar or greater cost does not.*

You also wrote: *"You are explicitly authorized to substantially redesign, simplify, replace, or retire UTRAP if the evidence indicates that it cannot reach this objective within our resources."* **This report is that evidence.**

Requirements 1 and 2 are solved by published work and were never the difficulty. Requirement 3 is the open problem.

# Five families: how each works, what we tried, why it failed

We did not run any method once at default settings. Each was swept, and in two cases we repaired the published objective before testing it.

## Family 1 — re-point the concept (edit methods)

| method | how it works | what we swept | why it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UCE** | closed-form least-squares edit of the cross-attention key/value matrices, re-mapping the concept's text embedding to a different output. No training | λ across three orders of magnitude, preserve-scale, and the **embedding selection** — upstream edits one embedding, we edit a range, which is the only reason our arm erases at all | the UNet's visual machinery is **untouched by construction**. The attacker re-points the mapping back. Falls in **4 gradient steps** |
| **ESD** | fine-tunes the UNet so the concept's conditional score moves toward the negative-guidance direction | training steps, learning rate, three independent build seeds | the capability survives; only the score shifts. Falls in **1 gradient step**, and returns with **no attacker at all** |

## Family 2 — make the model hard to fine-tune (immunisation, meta-learning)

| method | how it works | what we swept | why it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IMMA** | bi-level: an inner loop simulates a fine-tuner, the outer loop makes that fine-tuning ineffective | α from 1e-5 to 1e-7, 300–1500 steps — finding a clean operating point at suppression 0.133, utility 0.942 | rehearses against a **generic** fine-tuner. Falls in **1 gradient step** to one that targets the concept |
| **GIFT** | gradient-aware immunisation against malicious fine-tuning, with safe-concept retention | β from 0.1 to 10.0, the full usable range | fully reversed at **every usable β**, including under **its own DreamBooth threat model** |
| **Meta-Unlearning** | a three-term objective designed so that fine-tuning **on the erased concept** degrades the model — a self-destruct trap | we found variant A omits **two of Eq. 8's three terms**, restored them, then swept λ; added common random numbers, fixed timesteps, micro-batched meta-gradients, Adam-ε throttling | the trap must fire on a gradient signal **benign data also produces**. It fires on normal use; at the one λ leaving the model usable it buys **zero** robustness over its own ablation. Measured drift fell **below the random-walk floor** |
| **UTRAP** *(ours)* | train against unrolled inner relearning attacks so the path back is blocked | unroll depth K ∈ {0, 1, 8, 32}, λ sweeps, three arms | **18 of 18 attacks at 1.000**. The inner process differed from the real attacker on **seven axes**, so the hypothesis was never actually tested |

## Family 3 — delete the weights (pruning)

| method | how it works | what we swept | why it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FRP** | ranks weights by forget-importance minus retain-importance, zeroes the top ρ% | ρ, β, λ; plus a **mask-refill** experiment putting the mask back after the attack | **the mask is an initial condition, not a constraint.** Nothing holds the weights at zero, and the concept returned *through the weights FRP left alone* |

## Family 4 — destroy and rebuild (distillation)

| method | how it works | what we swept | why it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UNDO** | add noise to an erased model's weights, then distil the wreck back toward the erased teacher's outputs | α ∈ {0.3, 0.5}, four damage-targeting modes, teacher swaps, budget sweeps | **erasure and utility turn out to be the same objective.** At α=0.3 it erased *perfectly* (0.0000 at four eval seeds) and halved utility to 0.517; at α=0.5, utility fell to 0.292 |
| **selective damage** *(ours)* | weight the noise by retain-importance so damage spares what general generation needs | Fisher importance, its complement, and a **random control** | **it lost to the random control.** The importance signal the whole idea depends on is not there to find |

## Family 5 — make erasure a fixed point of the attack

| method | how it works | result |
|---|---|---|
| **ResAlign** | penalises *how much the erasure degrades after simulated fine-tuning*, back-propagating through the inner loop via implicit hypergradients | **holds.** See the dedicated section below |

# Why four of five families fail — from first principles

There are exactly three ways an attacker can fail, and every method is one of them:

1. **The information is not there** — nothing remains from which the concept can be cheaply rebuilt.
2. **It is there but unreachable** — no short path through the loss landscape leads back.
3. **Reaching it destroys the model** — every path passes through ruin.

**Route 1 fails because the concept is not separable.** The information needed to draw a church is largely a *subset* of the information needed to draw the retained concepts — arches, towers, masonry, perspective. You cannot delete a subset while preserving the superset. Our damage-then-distil arm is the proof: perfect erasure at 0.000, utility halved to 0.517. The two objectives are the same objective.

**Route 2 fails because the defender commits first.** We fix the weights at release; the attacker then sees everything and chooses learning rate, optimiser, data and which parameters to touch. The defence must hold against every attack; the attack need work once. And fine-tuning toward the concept overlaps with fine-tuning toward legitimate tasks, so blocking one blocks the other.

**Route 3 fails because the trigger is not separable either.** A trap must fire on a gradient signal benign data also produces. Either it is too sensitive and fires during ordinary use — which we measured — or the attacker steps around it.

## The compositional argument

A generative model does not store concepts; it composes them. A church is a reachable point in a space closed under composition, not an entry in a table. **You cannot remove the number seven from arithmetic while keeping three, four and addition.**

That is why the attacker needs only 25 steps and 768 scalars. The capability was never removed — only the binding between the word and the composition was moved, and a binding is cheap to restore.

## What we can and cannot claim

We can claim, on 164 arms and 1,215 measurements, that **post-hoc weight-level concept erasure in these architectures does not survive relearning, that the attacker's cost is trivially small, and that the cause is the non-separability of the target from the retained set.**

We **cannot** claim unlearning is impossible in principle. Three escapes remain, and ResAlign occupies one of them.

# The exception: ResAlign

ResAlign (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2507.16302) is the only published method in this area that demonstrates resistance to anything. We obtained **the authors' released checkpoints** — not a re-implementation — and tested them.

## Their claim reproduces exactly

Their protocol: `unsafe.csv`, 3 images per prompt, 30 inference steps, their MHSC "sexual" detector (ViT-L-14/openai backbone plus their trained projection head), threshold 0.5.

| arm | US rate | mean detector score |
|---|---:|---:|
| **ResAlign higher-safety** | **0.0000** (0/600) | 0.0143 |
| **base SD-1.4** *(positive control)* | **0.1817** (109/600) | 0.2053 |

Their reported un-attacked figure is 0.0000. **We measure 0.0000.**

**The control is why this means anything.** A detector reading zero on a defended model proves nothing unless it reads clearly non-zero on an undefended one. Base SD-1.4, through the identical pipeline, reads 0.1817 — 109 of 600 images flagged, mean score 14× higher. The instrument works and the zero is real.

## It survives a targeted attack

Their paper tests *benign downstream fine-tuning*. We tested an adversary who wants the concept back and trains directly on it — the threat every other arm here was measured against. Attacker trains on base generations of 40 prompts; scored only on 60 prompts it never saw.

| budget | learning rate | US after attack | % of base rate | attack valid? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 25 steps | 1e-5 | **0.0000** | 0% | yes |
| 25 steps | 5e-5 | **0.0250** | 14% | yes |
| 25 steps | 1e-4 | **0.0333** | 18% | yes |
| 100 steps | 1e-4 | **0.0333** | 18% | yes |
| **300 steps** | 1e-4 | **0.1333** | **73%** | yes |

Every cell valid — probe loss fell, weights moved well past the fp32 floor. **This is a defence holding, not a failed attack.**

**ResAlign is not an absolute defence, and that is the more useful result.** At 25 steps — the budget that drives every other arm to full recovery — it reaches 0.0333 against a base rate of 0.1817, retaining about 82% of its protection. Quadrupling to 100 steps buys the attacker nothing. By 300 steps it breaks, reaching 73% of base.

So the quantity is a **cost**: ResAlign makes the attack roughly **100× more expensive** than against any other method, which fall at one to four steps. That is an explicitly measured trade-off — precisely the form the mandate accepts — rather than a claim of permanence.

## What it does differently

Their outer objective, from their code, is one line:

```
loss = esd_loss(θ) + λ · [esd_loss(θ*) − esd_loss(θ)] + w · retain_loss
```

where **θ\*** is the model *after* a simulated fine-tuning run. In plain terms: *be erased now; don't become less erased after someone fine-tunes you; don't break everything else.*

The middle term is the entire contribution. They directly penalise **how much erasure degrades under fine-tuning**, and differentiate through the simulated fine-tuning with implicit hypergradients.

**This is categorically different from a trap.** Meta-Unlearning booby-traps the model: fine-tune on the concept and it self-destructs. That requires **detecting the attacker**, and the trigger signal is the same signal benign data produces — which is exactly the failure we measured. ResAlign never detects anything. It makes erasure a **fixed point** of fine-tuning.

Three further things they get right:

- **They harden against a distribution, not a point.** Fifteen inner configurations — learning rates {1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4}, inner steps {5, 10, 20, 30}, optimizers {Adam, GD}, methods {LoRA, DreamBooth, standard} — sampled three at random per training step. IMMA and UTRAP each rehearsed against one.
- **They anneal the pressure.** λ climbs 0.2 → 0.8 on a sigmoid: erase first, then progressively weight post-fine-tuning survival.
- **Implicit hypergradients, not unrolling.** Fixed-point iteration lets them differentiate through 30 inner steps. Our UTRAP unrolled K ∈ {0,1,8,32} and depth bought nothing.

**One detail strengthens the result rather than weakening it.** Their simulated attacker is LoRA rank-4, plus DreamBooth and standard fine-tuning. Our attack was a **full fine-tune of all 859M parameters** — out of distribution for what they trained against — and it still barely moved them. They generalised beyond their own training distribution.

# The measurement problem

The second pillar is that the standard metric cannot distinguish erasure from damage, so positive results in this area are difficult to falsify as currently reported.

Suppression is scored as a 12-way closed-set CLIP accuracy over 15 images **with no reject option**. Every image is forced onto one of twelve labels. On 27 October we generated the images behind our own numbers and looked at them, and found all three of these:

1. the concept genuinely gone, something coherent in its place;
2. degenerate texture on the target prompt;
3. the concept **plainly present** but carrying an artifact the classifier misreads.

A result we had built on for days — an apparent robustness advantage — was a classifier being fooled by a noise overlay on a model that still drew churches perfectly. The companion utility metric cannot catch this either, because it is measured only on non-target prompts and never inspects the target image.

This also explains an inversion. Judged by the metric, our arm erased better than UCE. Judged by the images, UCE removes the church entirely and ours leaves it plainly visible. **The ranking reverses when you look.**

## This criticism does not apply to ResAlign

Their metric is a **trained binary detector with a threshold**, not a closed-set argmax — and we verified its positive control. The defect we document does not touch their work, and **their evaluation is sounder than the one we have been criticising.** Any write-up must say so rather than sweep the field with one brush.

## A related conflation

"Robust concept erasure" names two different threats, routinely confused:

| tier | attacker may change | papers |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Prompt / embedding** | **nothing** — weights frozen | STEREO (CVPR 2025), AEGIS |
| **2. Benign downstream fine-tuning** | weights, on **unrelated** data | ResAlign |
| **3. Targeted relearning** | weights, on **the concept itself** | IMMA, GIFT, Meta-Unlearning claim this; our programme measures it |

A method hardened at tier 1 can fall at tier 3 without its paper being wrong. STEREO's attacker cannot touch a single weight.

# The contribution, and why it is publishable

**The proposed paper:**

> **Concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion is a rebinding, not a removal — four families of defence fail for one structural reason, a fifth escapes it, and the field's instruments cannot tell the difference.**

**It is a diagnosis, not a tally.** Negative results already exist for UCE and ESD, and the representation argument has been made for classifiers. What is missing is *why*. We give a mechanism, and the mechanism **predicts the numbers**: why 25 steps suffice, why 768 scalars suffice, why Fisher importance could not beat a random control, why damage-then-distil erases perfectly while halving utility.

**It invalidates an instrument the field depends on.** Not "these methods are weaker than reported" but "the standard measurement cannot distinguish erasure from damage" — supported by images rather than another accuracy number.

**The coverage does not exist elsewhere.** 164 arms, nine ported methods, two authors' checkpoints, six defence families, five concepts, three base models, 1,215 attack measurements — including the four methods built *specifically* to resist fine-tuning, which no one has put on a single bench under one attacker with honest cell accounting.

**It answers a question another group has publicly posed.** The AISTATS 2026 *Illusion of Unlearning* authors name extension to diffusion models as open future work.

**The method failures are evidence, not embarrassment.** Five directions built and closed, each by a rule written before it ran, **all failing for the same structural reason** — five independent probes of one obstruction. Our own method failing identically to the published ones is the strongest demonstration that this is not a matter of effort.

**And it now contains a positive result.** ResAlign works, we verified it rigorously, and we can state the mechanism that separates it from everything that fails.
