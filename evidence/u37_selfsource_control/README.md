# U37 — the control that closes U34's confound, and splits its claim in two

**2026-10-28. 6 of 6 cells valid, n=3 per arm, no failures.**

U34 fine-tuned the erased models on non-target images **generated by the base model** and found the
erased concept returned. Its own limits named the loophole: the training targets are base-model
behaviour, so the run might be pulling the model back toward base generally — including un-erasing —
rather than showing that training on unrelated concepts recovers the concept.

U37 removes the loophole. The downstream set is generated **by the erased model under test**.
Nothing in training comes from any model that can draw a church.

## Result

| arm | seed | U34: base-sourced | **U37: self-sourced** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `esd_church` | 0 | 1.0000 | **0.8000** |
| `esd_church` | 1 | 0.9333 | **0.8667** |
| `esd_church` | 6 | 0.8000 | **0.9333** |
| `uce_church` | 0 | 0.4667 | **0.0000** |
| `uce_church` | 1 | 0.6000 | **0.0667** |
| `uce_church` | 6 | 0.4000 | **0.2000** |

**ESD is unchanged.** Recovery survives the control at 0.80–0.93. Fine-tuning on golf balls, french
horns, cassette players and gas pumps — rendered by a model that cannot draw a church — still brings
churches back. **The confound is dead and U34's claim stands for ESD**, now on two independent
lines: the control here, and the image verification in U36.

**UCE collapses to approximately zero.** Its apparent recovery in U34 **was** base-model leakage.
U36 reached the same conclusion from the images, where UCE's "recovered" outputs were houses and
offices rather than churches. Two independent methods agree, so the UCE half of U34 is **withdrawn**.

## This produces the project's first resistance result, and it is not ours

**UCE resists benign downstream fine-tuning.** Across three seeds with the confound removed, its
erasure holds at 0.000–0.200. That is the first time anything in 164 arms has resisted anything.

It is a published method, not ours, and it resists only the weaker threat:

| threat | UCE | ESD |
|---|---|---|
| **benign downstream fine-tuning** (tier 2) | **holds** — 0.000–0.200 | **fails** — 0.800–0.933 |
| **targeted relearning** (tier 3) | fails in **4 gradient steps** | fails in **1 gradient step** |

**The three-tier attacker taxonomy is now an empirical finding rather than a framing device.** The
tiers separate a real method on real measurements: UCE survives ordinary downstream use and falls to
four steps of deliberate retraining.

## Why this matters for the write-up

It removes the strongest objection available to a reviewer — that our benign result was an artifact
of how the downstream set was built — and it does so by a control we ran on ourselves before
publishing, not after.

It also sharpens the central claim. The paper no longer says *nothing resists anything*. It says
**no method resists targeted relearning, the cheapest successful attack costs one to four gradient
steps, and the one method that resists ordinary use does so at a suppression level the standard
metric cannot distinguish from a broken model** (U29/U30: UCE at 0.0000 emits texture mush).

## Limits

- Church only, SD-1.4, 200 steps, 32 downstream images, two arms, n=3.
- The self-sourced downstream images inherit the erased model's own degradation; for UCE that means
  training on partly degenerate renders, which is itself a difference from a real downstream task.
- The U37 target panels have **not** been image-audited. UCE's non-recovery is a low number from the
  same metric that U29/U30 showed can be satisfied by a broken model, so "UCE resists" should be
  read as *suppression persists*, not as *the model remains good and churchless*, until inspected.
