with fresh-batch certified acceptance — derivation

Status.

Every ingredient below is standard (Section 7 lists them). What is offered is (i) a precise statement of which retention guarantees survive the actual AdamW update used by native GEM (LoRA, no retention term; agents/A_gem_code_facts.md §5–6), (ii) a strict first-order separation between the Adam-metric correction and its closest published primitive (Euclidean projection of the Adam update, Adam-NSCL-style; UNVERIFIED source), and (iii) a finite-step drift bound for one corrected step that makes the sketch, ridge, staleness and curvature errors explicit. The results are about a linearised retained map plus explicitly bounded remainders; they say nothing about image semantics, object removal, or FLUX.

Notation and exact objects

\(\theta\in\mathbb{R}^P\): trainable LoRA parameters (all \(A,B\) factors flattened), base weights frozen, \(\theta_0\) the initialisation (\(B=0\)). \(L_e(\theta)\): the native GEM erasure loss (detached teachers), \(g=\nabla L_e(\theta)\). Retained map \(f_r:\mathbb{R}^P\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\) stacks the student outputs on a retained probe batch \(R_1\) (\(n=N_r d\)); \(J=\partial f_r(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times P}\) (never formed densely at scale). AdamW (torch semantics, \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\epsilon,\lambda_{wd}\), step \(t\)): \(m'=\beta_1 m+(1-\beta_1)g\), \(v'=\beta_2 v+(1-\beta_2)g^{\odot2}\), \(\hat m=m'/(1-\beta_1^t)\), \(\hat v=v'/(1-\beta_2^t)\), \[D=\operatorname{diag}(d),\quad d_i=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hat v_i}+\epsilon}>0,\qquad \Delta_0=-\eta\,D\hat m-\eta\lambda_{wd}\theta\quad(\text{the actual step}).\] Sketch: \(\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}\) with i.i.d. \(\mathcal N(0,1/k)\) entries, \(S=\Sigma J\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times P}\) (computed with \(k\) VJPs). Metric \(H=D^{-1}\), \(\|x\|_H^2=x^\top D^{-1}x\). Candidate correction (exact, with ridge \(\mu\ge0\)): \[\Delta_1=\Delta_0-DS^\top (SDS^\top+\mu I)^{-1}S\Delta_0 . \tag{1}\] For \(\mu=0\) and \(S\) of full row rank write \(\Pi_D=I-DS^\top(SDS^\top)^{-1}S\); Euclidean analogue \(\Pi=I-S^\top(SS^\top)^{-1}S\). Pre-optimizer projection (GPM/OGD-style) feeds \(\Pi g\) into AdamW instead of \(g\). Idealised statements set \(\mu=0\), \(\Sigma=I\) (exact \(J\)) and fresh \(J\); Section 5 removes each idealisation.

Pre-optimizer projection is not retention-neutral under AdamW (Agent C, T1)

Proposition 1. Fix \(J\) and suppose every gradient fed to AdamW lies in \(\mathcal N=\operatorname{null}(J)\) (pre-projection). Then the momentum \(\hat m\in\mathcal N\) and the first-order retained change of the step \(\Delta_0\) is \(J\Delta_0=-\eta JD\hat m-\eta\lambda_{wd}J\theta\). (a) \(JD\hat m=0\) for every \(\hat m\in\mathcal N\) iff \(D\mathcal N\subseteq\mathcal N\). (b) For every \(c\in\mathbb{R}\), \(\|JD\hat m\|\le\|J\|\,\|D-cI\|\,\|\hat m\|\); with \(c=(d_{\max}+d_{\min})/2\) this gives \(\|JD\hat m\|\le\|J\|\frac{d_{\max}-d_{\min}}{2}\|\hat m\|\). (c) The weight-decay term \(\eta\lambda_{wd}J\theta\) is untouched by any gradient projection.

Proof. \(\hat m\) is a linear combination of past (projected) gradients, all in the subspace \(\mathcal N\), so \(\hat m\in\mathcal N\). (a) \(JD\hat m=0\ \forall \hat m\in\mathcal N\) iff \(D\mathcal N\subseteq\operatorname{null}(J)=\mathcal N\). (b) \(J\hat m=0\) gives \(JD\hat m=J(D-cI)\hat m\); take norms; \(\|D-cI\|=\max_i|d_i-c|\), minimised at the midrange. (c) The term does not depend on \(g\). ◻

Remark 2. Since \(D\) is diagonal with (generically) distinct entries, \(D\)-invariant subspaces are sums of coordinate subspaces; a null space that is rotated relative to the coordinate axes is generically not invariant. At AdamW step 1, \(\hat m/\sqrt{\hat v}=\operatorname{sign}(g)\) entrywise (\(|g_i|\gg\epsilon\)), so the retained change is \(\eta\|J\operatorname{sign}(\Pi g)\|\), which does not shrink as the projection becomes more accurate.

The Adam-metric correction: characterisation and a strict separation

Proposition 3 (trust-region characterisation; standard). Let \(D\succ0\), \(S\) full row rank, \(r>0\), \(\hat m=g\) (e.g. \(\beta_1=0\) or AdamW step 1) and \(\lambda_{wd}=0\). The problem \[\max_x\ -g^\top x\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Sx=0,\ \|x\|_H\le r\] has the unique solution \(x^\star=-r\,\Pi_D Dg/\|\Pi_D Dg\|_H\) whenever \(\Pi_D Dg\ne0\), and the optimal value is \(r\|\Pi_D Dg\|_H\). Moreover \[-g^\top(-\Pi_D Dg)=\|(I-Q)D^{1/2}g\|^2\ \ge 0,\qquad Q=D^{1/2}S^\top(SDS^\top)^{-1}SD^{1/2}, \tag{2}\] with equality iff \(g\in\operatorname{range}(S^\top)\). Also \(\|\Pi_D w\|_H\le\|w\|_H\) for all \(w\), and \(S\Pi_D=0\).

Proof. Substitute \(x=D^{1/2}y\): maximise \(-(D^{1/2}g)^\top y\) s.t. \((SD^{1/2})y=0\), \(\|y\|\le r\). The feasible set is the ball intersected with \(\operatorname{null}(SD^{1/2})\), whose orthogonal projector is \(I-Q\) (\(Q\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(D^{1/2}S^\top)\)). By Cauchy–Schwarz on that subspace the unique maximiser is \(y^\star=-r(I-Q)D^{1/2}g/\|(I-Q)D^{1/2}g\|\), and \(D^{1/2}(I-Q)D^{1/2}g=\Pi_D Dg\), giving \(x^\star\). Then \(g^\top\Pi_D Dg=(D^{1/2}g)^\top(I-Q)(D^{1/2}g)=\|(I-Q)D^{1/2}g\|^2\) since \(I-Q\) is an orthogonal projector. Equality iff \(D^{1/2}g\in\operatorname{range}(D^{1/2}S^\top)\) iff \(g\in\operatorname{range}(S^\top)\). In \(y\)-coordinates \(\Pi_D\) acts as the orthogonal projector \(I-Q\), hence non-expansive in \(\|\cdot\|_H\); \(S\Pi_D=S-SDS^\top(SDS^\top)^{-1}S=0\). ◻

Proposition 4 (strict separation from Euclidean post-projection). For the Euclidean correction \(\Delta_E=\Pi\Delta_0\) of the same AdamW step (\(\hat m=g\), \(\lambda_{wd}=0\)), the first-order erasure progress \(-g^\top\Delta_E=\eta\,g^\top\Pi Dg\) can be negative; the Adam-metric progress \(-g^\top\Pi_D\Delta_0=\eta\,g^\top\Pi_DDg\) never is (Prop. 3). The two coincide when \(D=cI\). Explicit instance: \(P=2\), \(S=[1,1]\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(0.01,1)\), \(g=(1,0.5)\): \(g^\top\Pi Dg=\tfrac12(d_1g_1-d_2g_2)(g_1-g_2)=\tfrac12(-0.49)(0.5)=-0.1225<0\), while \(g^\top\Pi_DDg=\|(I-Q)D^{1/2}g\|^2=\frac{d_1d_2(g_1-g_2)^2}{d_1+d_2}=\frac{0.01\cdot0.25}{1.01}\approx0.002475>0\).

Proof. For \(S=[1,1]\), \(\Pi=\frac12\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{psmallmatrix}\), so \(\Pi Dg=\frac12(d_1g_1-d_2g_2)(1,-1)^\top\) and \(g^\top\Pi Dg=\frac12(d_1g_1-d_2g_2)(g_1-g_2)\). For the metric version, \(SDS^\top=d_1+d_2\) and \(\Pi_DDg=Dg-\frac{d_1g_1+d_2g_2}{d_1+d_2}(d_1,d_2)^\top=\frac{d_1d_2(g_1-g_2)}{d_1+d_2}(1,-1)^\top\), so \(g^\top\Pi_DDg=\frac{d_1d_2(g_1-g_2)^2}{d_1+d_2}\). Substitute. If \(D=cI\) then \(\Pi_D=\Pi\). ◻

Remark 5 (what Prop. 4 does not say). It does not say the metric correction always yields more progress: with \(g=(1,-0.5)\) in the same instance the Euclidean progress is larger (0.3825 vs 0.0223 in units of \(\eta\); code/scratch_checks/sign_check.py). Prop. 3 gives optimality only at equal \(H\)-norm (Adam’s own trust region). Comparisons must therefore be made at tuned or matched step size — the protocol does so. Under random draws (development only) the Euclidean sign failure occurred in 3.5 % of cases (same script).

Counterexample 6 (momentum). With \(\hat m\ne g\), \(-g^\top\Pi_D D\hat m\) can be negative (take \(\hat m\) dominated by an old gradient opposite to \(g\) outside \(\operatorname{range}(S^\top)\)). Hence the rule “if \(g^\top\Delta_1\ge0\) set \(\Delta_1=0\)”; Prop. 3’s sign guarantee covers only \(\beta_1=0\) or \(\hat m\parallel g\) on \(\operatorname{null}(S)\)-relevant directions.

Remark 7 (weight decay). Because (1) acts on the whole \(\Delta_0\), the first-order retained change of the decoupled weight-decay term is also removed (\(S\Delta_1=0\) exactly for \(\mu=0\)), unlike Prop. 1(c). For LoRA the weight-decay map change is exactly \(((1-\eta\lambda)^2-1)BA\) (both factors scale), whose second-order part is not removed.

Exact finite-step behaviour of one corrected step

Let \(\theta^+=\theta+s\Delta_1\). The exact retained change is \(f_r(\theta^+)-f_r(\theta)\); write the full (unsketched) Jacobian at the current point \(J_\theta\) and at the probe refresh point \(J_{\theta_s}\) (staleness). Assumptions: (A1) \(\|J_\theta-J_{\theta_s}\|\le L_J\|\theta-\theta_s\|\); (A2) along the segment \([\theta,\theta^+]\) the second derivative of \(f_r\) is bounded: \(\|f_r(\theta+h)-f_r(\theta)-J_\theta h\|\le\frac{\beta}{2}\|h\|^2\).

Lemma 8 (ridge residual). With \(S\) any \(k\times P\) matrix, (1) gives \(S\Delta_1=\mu(SDS^\top+\mu I)^{-1}S\Delta_0\), hence \(\|S\Delta_1\|\le\frac{\mu}{\lambda_{\min}(SDS^\top)+\mu}\|S\Delta_0\|\).

Proof. \(S\Delta_1=S\Delta_0-SDS^\top(G+\mu I)^{-1}S\Delta_0\) with \(G=SDS^\top\); \(I-G(G+\mu I)^{-1}=\mu(G+\mu I)^{-1}\); the eigenvalues of \(\mu(G+\mu I)^{-1}\) are \(\mu/(\lambda_i+\mu)\). ◻

Lemma 9 (sketch residual). Let \(J=J_{\theta_s}\) have singular values \(\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\dots\), \(\Sigma\) Gaussian \(k\times n\), and \(Y=\operatorname{range}(J^\top\Sigma^\top)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^P\). Every \(x\) with \(\Sigma Jx=0\) satisfies \(x\perp Y\) and \(\|Jx\|\le E_\Sigma\|x\|\) with \(E_\Sigma=\|J(I-P_Y)\|\). (a) If \(k\ge\operatorname{rank}J\) then \(E_\Sigma=0\) almost surely. (b) For \(k=k_0+p\), \(p\ge2\), the randomized range-finder bound (Halko–Martinsson–Tropp 2011, §10, average spectral error theorem; applied to \(A=J^\top\), \(\Omega=\Sigma^\top\)) gives \(\mathbb{E}E_\Sigma\le\bigl(1+\sqrt{k_0/(p-1)}\bigr)\sigma_{k_0+1}+\frac{e\sqrt{k}}{p}\bigl(\sum_{j>k_0}\sigma_j^2\bigr)^{1/2}\), so by Markov \(\Pr(E_\Sigma>t\,\mathbb{E}E_\Sigma)\le1/t\). Always \(E_\Sigma\ge\sigma_{k+1}(J)\): the bound is informative only for fast spectral decay, and it concerns the probe batch \(R_1\) only (review F).

Proof. \(\Sigma Jx=0\iff x\perp\operatorname{range}(J^\top\Sigma^\top)=Y\), so \(x=(I-P_Y)x\) and \(\|Jx\|=\|J(I-P_Y)x\|\le E_\Sigma\|x\|\). (a) A Gaussian \(\Sigma^\top\) restricted to \(\operatorname{range}(J)\) (dimension \(\operatorname{rank}J\le k\)) is a.s. of full rank \(\operatorname{rank}J\), so \(Y=\operatorname{range}(J^\top)\) and \(J(I-P_Y)=0\). (b) \(\|J(I-P_Y)\|=\|(I-P_Y)J^\top\|\), which is exactly the quantity bounded in HMT §10 for \(A=J^\top\) with Gaussian test matrix \(\Omega=\Sigma^\top\) (scaling of \(\Omega\) does not change \(Y\)). The constant is quoted from that theorem from memory (UNVERIFIED theorem number in this sprint; arXiv unreachable); Markov’s inequality gives the tail. Note: the bound requires \(\Sigma\) independent of \(J\), which holds because \(\Sigma\) is drawn fresh at each refresh before \(J\)-products are taken; it does not require independence from \(x\), because the bound is uniform over \(x\perp Y\). ◻

Theorem 10 (finite-step retained drift of one VAU step). Under (A1)–(A2), with \(S=\Sigma J_{\theta_s}\) and \(\Delta_1\) from (1), \[\|f_r(\theta+s\Delta_1)-f_r(\theta)\|\ \le\ s\Bigl(E_\Sigma\|\Delta_1\|+\kappa_\Sigma\|S\Delta_1\|\Bigr) + s\,L_J\|\theta-\theta_s\|\,\|\Delta_1\| + \tfrac{\beta}{2}s^2\|\Delta_1\|^2 ,\] where \(\|S\Delta_1\|\) is bounded by Lemma 8 (zero if \(\mu=0\)), \(E_\Sigma\) by Lemma 9 (zero a.s. if \(k\ge\operatorname{rank}J_{\theta_s}\)), and \(\kappa_\Sigma=1/\sigma_{\min}(\Sigma|_V)\) with \(V=J_{\theta_s}Y\), finite (shown in the proof).

Proof. Write \(f_r(\theta+h)-f_r(\theta)=J_\theta h+\rho\) with \(\|\rho\|\le\frac\beta2\|h\|^2\) (A2), \(h=s\Delta_1\). Split \(J_\theta h=J_{\theta_s}h+(J_\theta-J_{\theta_s})h\); the second term is bounded by (A1). Decompose \(h=h_\perp+h_Y\) with \(h_\perp\perp Y\), \(h_Y\in Y\) (\(\|h_\perp\|,\|h_Y\|\le\|h\|\)). Then \(\|J_{\theta_s}h_\perp\|\le E_\Sigma\|h\|\) (Lemma 9) and \(Sh=\Sigma J_{\theta_s}h_Y\) because \(\Sigma J_{\theta_s}h_\perp=0\). \(J_{\theta_s}h_Y\in V=J_{\theta_s}Y=\operatorname{range}(J_{\theta_s}J_{\theta_s}^\top\Sigma^\top)\). \(\Sigma\) is injective on \(V\): if \(z=J_{\theta_s}J_{\theta_s}^\top\Sigma^\top w\) and \(\Sigma z=0\) then \(w^\top\Sigma z=\|J_{\theta_s}^\top\Sigma^\top w\|^2=0\), so \(z=0\). Hence \(\sigma_{\min}(\Sigma|_V)>0\) and \(\|J_{\theta_s}h_Y\|\le\kappa_\Sigma\|Sh\|\). If \(\mu=0\) then \(Sh=0\). Combine with the triangle inequality. ◻

Remark 11. The bound is on the probe batch \(R_1\) only. In zero-init LoRA under AdamW the \(\beta\|\Delta_1\|^2\) term dominates and the metric correction can increase \(\|\Delta_1\|\) by up to \(\sqrt{d_{\max}/d_{\min}}\) (review F, f5: \(>99.9\,\%\) of the actual per-step retained change was the bilinear term). Theorem 10 is a prediction-side bound: every constant (\(E_\Sigma\), \(L_J\), \(\beta\)) is unknown for a real network. That is why the candidate does not rely on it for acceptance; it relies on Theorem 13.

Counterexample 12 (LoRA gauge; outside the first-order guarantee). For a LoRA layer \(W+BA\) and any \(K\), the step \(dB=BK\), \(dA=-KA\) has first-order map change \(dB\,A+B\,dA=0\) for every input, so it passes any exact first-order constraint, yet the exact change is \(dB\,dA=-BK^2A\ne0\) (Agent C, C-X2; computed 2.3 % of \(\|BAx\|\) at \(\|K\|=0.3\)). Only an evaluation of the actual finite step detects it.

Certified acceptance under adaptivity, with optimizer-state rollback

Clipped per-sample drift \(r_x(\theta)=\min(\|f_x(\theta)-f_x(\theta_0)\|^2,M)\in[0,M]\); population \(\rho_M(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal R}r_x(\theta)\) over the retained distribution \(\mathcal R\).

Theorem 13 (gate). Run any update rule for \(T\) steps with at most \(R\) trials per step. At each trial \(j\) draw a fresh batch \(R_2^{(j)}\) of \(n_2\) i.i.d. samples from \(\mathcal R\), independent of everything used to construct the trial point \(\vartheta_j\) (conditionally on the history), and accept iff \(\hat\rho_j+c\le\tau^2\), \(\hat\rho_j=\frac1{n_2}\sum_{x\in R_2^{(j)}}r_x(\vartheta_j)\), \(c=M\sqrt{\ln(TR/\delta)/(2n_2)}\). Then with probability at least \(1-\delta\) every accepted iterate satisfies \(\rho_M\le\tau^2\). If additionally \(\|f_x(\theta)-f_x(\theta_0)\|^2\le M\) a.s. along the run, \(\rho_M\) is the true mean drift. The cap \(TR\) and \(\tau,M,\delta,n_2\) must be fixed in advance; the \(R_2\) draws must be fresh i.i.d. and never reused; the certified quantity is the clipped mean \(\rho_M\) under the verification sampling law. Rejected trials leave \((\theta,m,v,c)\) unchanged; accepted steps commit the AdamW state computed from the raw \(g\) while \(\theta\) moves by \(s\Delta_1\), so the trajectory is not an AdamW trajectory.

Proof. Let \(\mathcal F_{j}\) be the \(\sigma\)-field of everything before batch \(j\) is drawn; \(\vartheta_j\) is \(\mathcal F_j\)-measurable and \(R_2^{(j)}\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_j\). Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_j\), the \(r_x(\vartheta_j)\) are i.i.d. in \([0,M]\) with mean \(\rho_M(\vartheta_j)\), so Hoeffding gives \(\Pr(\rho_M(\vartheta_j)>\hat\rho_j+c\mid\mathcal F_j)\le\delta/(TR)\). The bad event “some accepted \(\vartheta_j\) has \(\rho_M>\tau^2\)” is contained in \(\bigcup_j\{\rho_M(\vartheta_j)>\hat\rho_j+c\}\) (acceptance means \(\hat\rho_j+c\le\tau^2\)); at most \(TR\) trials, so the union bound gives \(\le\delta\); the number of trials actually used may be random and adaptive, because the union is over the fixed cap of \(TR\) potential trials (unused trials contribute nothing). The clipping statement is immediate. Rollback: a rejected trial restores \((\theta,m,v,c)\) bitwise, so no rejected trial influences later state. ◻

Counterexample 14 (adaptivity; why \(R_1\ne R_2\)). If the trial point is constructed in the null space of the same batch used to verify it, the empirical drift can be zero while population drift is not (Agent C computed \(3\cdot10^{-30}\) vs \(1.1\cdot10^{-3}\)). Theorem 13 requires independence.

Counterexample 15 (rare subgroup). The gate certifies a mean. A retained subgroup of mass \(q\) is absent from \(n_2\) samples with probability \((1-q)^{n_2}\) (\(0.37\) for \(q=0.005\), \(n_2=200\)); its drift is uncontrolled.

Limiting cases

(i) \(D=cI\) (e.g. SGD, or AdamW when all \(|g_i|\) equal): metric and Euclidean post-projection coincide, and (for \(\beta_1=0\), \(\lambda_{wd}=0\), and if the pre- and post-projection runs share \(D\)) so does pre-projection. (ii) \(S=0\) (no probes): the correction is skipped by specification (the ridge solve would be singular), \(\Delta_1=\Delta_0\), and VAU-noGate is AdamW bitwise. (iii) \(g\in\operatorname{range}(S^\top)\): \(\Delta_1\) has zero first-order progress (Prop. 3); rejected. (iv) Rich retained set, \(\operatorname{rank}J=P\), with exact \(J\) (\(k\ge\operatorname{rank}J\)): the exact null space is \(\{0\}\), \(\Delta_1\approx0\) (ridge only) — VAU erases nothing; a soft penalty or the gate alone is then the only meaningful arm. (v) \(\tau\to\infty\): the gate always accepts. (vi) \(\tau\to0\): only steps with zero clipped empirical drift and \(c\le\tau^2\) pass; for finite \(n_2\), \(c>0\), so nothing passes.

Why the factor-wise exact alternative was declined (coordinator; review F objection 2)

For one LoRA layer \(y=(W+BA)x\) and a step \((dA,dB)\) the exact change on inputs \(X\) is \((B+dB)(A+dA)X-BAX=dB\,AX+(B+dB)\,dA\,X\) (no remainder).

Proposition 16 (factor-wise exactness). Let \(U\) be an orthonormal basis with \(X\subseteq\operatorname{span}U\). If \(dA\,U=0\) and \(dB\,(AU)=0\) then the layer output on every column of \(X\) is unchanged, for every step size. If every LoRA layer of a feed-forward composition (including per-token q/k projections inside attention) satisfies this with \(U_\ell\) spanning that layer’s activations on the probed inputs at the current \(\theta\), the network outputs on the probed inputs are exactly unchanged, and so are all probed activations (the constraints stay valid at the next step for the same probes; only \(AU\) must be recomputed).

Proof. \(dA\,X=dA\,UU^\top X=0\) and \(dB\,AX=dB\,(AU)U^\top X=0\), so the exact change above vanishes. For the composition, induct in topological order: if all inputs of layer \(\ell\) on probed data are unchanged, its output is unchanged; non-adapted operations (softmax, normalisation, nonlinearities) are deterministic functions of unchanged inputs. ◻

Proposition 17 (collapse under zero-init \(B\)). If \(B=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(AU)=r\) (generic once \(\dim\operatorname{span}U\ge r\)), the constraints of Prop. 16 force \(dB=0\), and since \(dA\,U=0\) keeps \(AU\) unchanged, \(B=0\) for all later steps: the adapter output \(BAx\) is \(0\) for every input, so nothing is erased.

Proof. \(dB\,(AU)=0\) with \(AU\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times q}\) of rank \(r\) means the rows of \(dB\) are orthogonal to \(\mathbb{R}^r\), so \(dB=0\). \((A+dA)U=AU\), so the rank condition persists; by induction \(B\equiv0\). ◻

Remark 18 (the working construction is known). If instead the rows of \(A\) satisfy \(AU=0\) (and \(A\) is frozen or \(dA\,U=0\)), then \(BAX=0\) for every \(B\), so \(B\) trains freely with exact probed retention, and a target input \(x_t\) can change only through its residual \((I-UU^\top)x_t\). This is the null-A / InfLoRA-style construction (UNVERIFIED this sprint), close to null-space edits such as SPEED and AlphaEdit (UNVERIFIED). It is used as the control na_lora, not as a candidate. Held-out retained inputs are protected only through their component inside \(\operatorname{span}U\): for one layer the change is \(BA(I-UU^\top)x\), unbounded in general (computed: drift \(14.1\) on held-out inputs, factor_exact_check.py).

Standard ingredients versus what is specific here

Under the change of variables \(y=D^{-1/2}\theta\) the VAU correction is the Euclidean (Adam-NSCL/GPM-style) projection in Adam’s whitened coordinates. Standard: scaled/metric gradient projection and its trust-region form (Prop. 3; e.g. Bertsekas’ scaled gradient projection); null-space projection of an optimizer’s update for continual learning (Adam-NSCL; UNVERIFIED this sprint); GPM/OGD gradient projection; HMT randomized range finder (Lemma 9); Taylor/Lipschitz remainders (Theorem 10); Hoeffding with union bound and backtracking (Theorem 13; TRPO/CPO-style line search). Specific to this sprint (not claimed as a general contribution): the identification that native GEM’s verified training configuration (AdamW, zero-init LoRA, no retention) sits in the regime of Prop. 1; the explicit sign separation of Prop. 4 between the metric and Euclidean corrections of the same Adam step; the combination of those with Theorem 13’s rollback semantics; and the prospective, falsifiable predictions tested in results/. Whether any of this improves real erasure/preservation is unknown.
