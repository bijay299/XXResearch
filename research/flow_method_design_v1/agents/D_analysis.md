## 2. Results
### 2.1 Endpoint vs local (question 1)
* **D-T1 (DERIVED; COMPUTED check).** This is the exact nonlinear variation-of-constants formula. The endpoint error equals the integral of the local velocity error, **evaluated on the student's own states** and propagated by the **reference** flow-map Jacobian. The resulting bound has the explicit log-norm constant mu(-d_x u) = (-1 + (1-sigma) lambda_max(Cov[x0|x_sigma]) / sigma^2) / sigma.
* The Lipschitz-Gronwall version is loose whenever the denoising flow contracts. For Gaussian data with std s and the GEM window, the Gronwall bound divided by the actual error was **9.5x (s=.3), 79x (s=.1) and 870x (s=.03)**. The log-norm bound was exact (`D_bounds` S1a, rel. err < 2e-3 vs 4000-step Euler).
* **Prefix-only edits are contracted at the endpoint.** Suppose the edit acts only for sigma >= sigma1 and the late field is the frozen target teacher. Then the endpoint moves by at most s/sigma_tot(sigma1) times the state displacement. The discrete 28-step Flux schedule gives measured ratios 0.36, 0.10 and 0.016 against the continuous bounds 0.40, 0.13 and 0.04 (S1b).
  So a local erasure loss on GEM's prefix controls the endpoint only through (a) parameter sharing that carries the edit into steps 11..27, or (b) pushing the state across a basin boundary, where mu > 0.
* **Counterexample (nonlinear).** With bimodal data, the first-order (reference-state) prediction has a relative error of 86-94% once the velocity offset c >= 0.1 in the coarse case, and mode flips appear (S1c). Linear endpoint control fails near basin boundaries, which is exactly where erasure has to act.
* **Exposure / covariate shift inside the prefix is small by construction.** The drift is at most C_W * (1-sigma1) * sup||Delta|| = 0.228 C_W sup||Delta||, with C_W = 1 if lambda_max(Cov[x0|x_sigma]) <= 2.61 on the window (D-C2).
  In the toy, the measured endpoint scores for on-policy, teacher-rollout and noised-data states differed by at most about 0.1, within seed spread (S3 grid). This held for 3 cases x 3 parametrisations.

### 2.2 Importance sampling (question 2)
* **D-P2 (DERIVED).** p/q-weighted sampling of the 11 terms is unbiased exactly when q covers the support. Sampling without weights optimises J_q, a **different objective**.
  The variance-optimal q is proportional to p_k ||g_k|| in **parameter** space, and not to the contrast ||b-a||. GEM's output gradient norm |u+ - eta u-| is scale free (B-D7), so contrast-proportional q puts weight about 1/L_k on low-contrast steps. As L_k -> 0 with g_k != 0, the variance diverges.
* **COMPUTED (UNI, SHARED student, 10 iterations in).** Across the prefix, contrast is nearly flat (2.00-2.33) and the per-step gradients are nearly parallel. Relative trace variance of one-step IS: uniform 0.48%, q proportional to contrast 0.67% (**+40%, worse**), q proportional to gradient norm 0.45% (optimal, -5%).
  The unweighted contrast-q expectation has cosine 0.99999 to the true gradient. Training with deterministic contrast weights changed endpoint scores by <= 0.001.
* GEM sums all 11 steps deterministically, so it has **zero** timestep-sampling variance. IS can only save compute (5(11-m)F per iteration for m sampled steps) at the price of variance. Single-step IS at matched Adam steps erased less in the toy (0.77-0.97 vs 0.59-0.75).
* **Zero-set invariance (DERIVED).** If every per-step hinge can be zeroed at once, any positive reweighting has the same global minimiser set. Reweighting then matters only through the 23-step optimisation path and through conflicts under finite capacity.

### 2.4 Pre-registered predictions: outcomes
* P-D1 held, but it does not discriminate: LOCAL fails in every window.
* P-D2 was **FALSIFIED**. The coarse case did not erase with a LOCAL prefix. The measured commitment sigma* was 0.68 (coarse) and 0.13 (fine), both outside the window, so the premise was wrong too.
* P-D3 was **FALSIFIED** (SHARED prefix UNI scored 1.0 at lr .05).
* P-D4 is INCONCLUSIVE.
* The `rho` leakage metric in the S3 JSON is degenerate and must not be used: contrast is exactly 0 at sigma=1 when the teacher and anchor means coincide. The contrast and drift columns are valid.

## 3. Ideas discarded
| idea | why not a candidate |
|---|---|
| On-policy rollouts with refresh / DAgger | Already native GEM (D-F2). Stale refresh R saves at most 11(1-1/R)/66 <= 17% of forwards; the toy showed no consistent effect |
| Noised real/generated latents as states | Equal to teacher-rollout states in expectation when the teacher is exact (D-P1). For GEM it differs only through guidance 3.0 vs 1.0 (D-F4). The toy showed no material difference |
| q(t) proportional to teacher contrast | Either an objective change (unweighted) or a variance increase (weighted), by D-P2; COMPUTED +40% variance |
| Endpoint-sensitivity weighting w_k = ||d x0 / d x_k|| | This is the linearised endpoint (backprop-through-sampler / adjoint) objective. It is established reward-fine-tuning machinery (DRaFT/AlignProp-type, UNVERIFIED citations) and needs >= 17 extra fwd+bwd per weighted step. D-T1 shows the linearisation fails near basin boundaries |
| Wider / uniform window over all 28 steps | The only effect seen, but it is the simple alternative "uniform-t", and in GEM it is a hyperparameter change (`partial_denoising_end_at_timestep`, plus a random subset). TRACE (late-step restriction, UNVERIFIED) and EraseFlow (trajectory objective, UNVERIFIED) cover the idea space |
| Logit-normal t | GEM has no t sampling to replace. Any sigma density over student states is a window/weight choice under D-P2 |

## 4. Verdict
**NONE.** No timestep, coverage or endpoint candidate is materially distinct from GEM's native student-rollout prefix and from the simple alternatives.
What survives is a diagnosis plus a cheap real-model ablation: GEM's prefix covers only sigma >= 0.77, and D-T1 says prefix edits reach the endpoint only via timestep sharing or basin crossing. The ablation is end_at=27 with a random 11-of-28 subset at matched forwards (D_candidate_spec.md section 6).
